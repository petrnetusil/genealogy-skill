# Belgium – country guide

A practical guide for researching Belgian ancestors from family memories back to the 1600s (and with luck the
1500s). Read it together with `reference/registers.md` (how to read registers in general),
`reference/portals.md` (international portals) and `methodology/beyond_registers.md`.

Facts were checked against official pages in November 2026; every portal has a source in **Sources** at the end
(numbers in square brackets, e.g. [1]). Things that could not be verified are marked **(unverified)**. Portals
change – recheck terms the first time you use one in a project.

## In short

| Period | Main source | Where online | Account / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| today → c. 1920s | family papers, death notices, municipality (extracts) | mostly offline; ask the municipality | — |
| 1920 → 1796 | **civil registration** (*burgerlijke stand / état civil*) + 10-year indexes | State Archives **AGATHA** [1][2]; FamilySearch [8] | AGATHA: free account; FamilySearch: free account |
| 1846 → c. 1900 | **population registers** (*bevolkingsregisters / registres de population*) | partly FamilySearch catalog, some municipal sites [10][11] | varies |
| 1796 → c. 1580 | **parish registers** (*parochieregisters / registres paroissiaux*), mostly Latin | AGATHA – almost all digitised [2][3] | free account |
| any period | volunteer **indexes** ("Analyses of records", 42 million names) | AGATHA analyses search [4] | **no login needed** |
| when stuck | notarial deeds, *staten van goed*, aldermen's registers, militia, fiscal and land records | AGATHA archive inventories, regional societies | free / membership |

Three things make Belgium unusually good for beginners: civil registration starts already in 1796, the State
Archives has put almost all pre-1796 parish registers online free of charge, and marriage acts name the parents
of both partners – so you can usually climb a generation per marriage.

## 1. How Belgian records are organised

### Who keeps what
- **Municipalities** (*gemeente / commune*) create the civil registers and population registers. Civil registers
  are kept in **two originals**: one stays in the municipality, the duplicate goes to the registry of the
  **Court of First Instance** and later to the **State Archives** (*Rijksarchief / Archives de l'État*) [2][9].
- **The State Archives in Belgium** (Brussels head office + provincial repositories such as Antwerp-Beveren,
  Bruges, Ghent, Kortrijk, Leuven, Hasselt, Mons, Namur, Liège, Arlon, Eupen…) holds the duplicate civil
  registers, the parish registers before 1796, notarial and court archives, and old-regime archives.
  Their online platform is **AGATHA** (agatha.arch.be) [1].
- **City archives** of large towns keep their own copies and extra series: e.g. the Archives of the City of
  Brussels [12], FelixArchief (Antwerp), Archief Gent [13], Bruges, Leuven.
- **The 1977 mergers.** The State Archives organises everything by the **old municipalities before the 1977
  merger** (2,359 municipalities were reduced to 589). Search "Marche-lez-Écaussinnes", not "Écaussinnes" [2].
  Later mergers (2019, and in Flanders 2025) changed names again – always find the historical municipality first
  (unverified for the exact list of 2025 mergers).

### Civil registration (from 1796)
Introduced under French rule (Belgium was annexed in 1795). The first acts are in **French** and dated with the
**French Republican calendar** until the end of 1805 (see section 3). Many registers are split at 1823, when
Dutch-period language rules applied in Flemish municipalities [14]; after 1830 French again dominates in many
places, Dutch returns gradually in Flanders in the later 19th century (exact dates vary by municipality –
unverified in detail). In the East Cantons acts are in German (see "Regions").

| Record | Dutch / French name | Contents | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Birth act | *geboorteakte / acte de naissance* | child, date and hour, address, father (age, occupation), mother (maiden name), declarant, two witnesses | women always appear under their **maiden name** |
| Marriage act | *huwelijksakte / acte de mariage* | bride and groom (age, birthplace, residence, occupation), **parents of both** (alive/deceased, residence), earlier spouses, witnesses | the best first target – two families at once |
| Marriage annexes | *huwelijksbijlagen / annexes (pièces) de mariage* | birth/baptism extracts of the couple, death acts of parents or grandparents, earlier spouse's death, parental consent, militia certificate | can jump **two generations** at once; kept with the court duplicate. **Not digitised on AGATHA** [2]; some are on FamilySearch (e.g. the East Flanders collection contains "marriage supplements") [8] |
| Marriage banns | *huwelijksafkondigingen / publications de mariage* | intended marriage, parents | useful when the marriage took place elsewhere |
| Death act | *overlijdensakte / acte de décès* | deceased, age, birthplace, spouse, parents (often), declarants | ages are approximate – never take them as a birth date; normally **no cause of death** (French model; unverified for every period) |
| Annual index | *jaartafel / table annuelle* | names → act number | at the end of each yearly register |
| 10-year index | *tienjarige tafel / table décennale* | all births, marriages, deaths of a municipality for 10 years, alphabetical by surname or initial | start every search here [9] |

Divorces (rare before the 20th century) are noted in the margin of marriage acts and in the marriage register [9].

### Population registers (from 1846)
After the national census of 1846 every municipality kept a **population register** – a book of households,
renewed after each ten-yearly census (1856, 1866 …). Each household page lists name, birthplace and date,
civil status, occupation, relationship to the head, address, arrivals (from where) and departures (to where,
including "America") [3][10]. Some larger towns started registers already in the French period [3].
Separate **foreigners' registers** exist (and from 1933 a separate alien register) [10].
They tell you **where the family moved** – and therefore in which municipality to look for the next act.

### Parish registers (before 1796)
Baptisms, marriages and burials, mostly in simplified **Latin**, from the late 16th century (after the Council of
Trent) – the State Archives' search period starts at **1580** [2]. They give baptism and burial dates rather than
birth and death dates [3]. Gaps are common (war, fire, no resident priest, chapels of ease that sent entries to
the mother parish) [2][3]. About 28,000–30,000 registers survive for present-day Belgium; the complete set held
by the State Archives is digitised [1][2][3]. Protestant registers are rare; FamilySearch has "Belgium, French
Protestant Parish Registers, 1500-1858" [8].

### Access rules and closing periods (current)
| Source | Public after | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Birth acts (incl. indexes) | **100 years** | [2] |
| Marriage acts (incl. indexes) | **75 years** | law of 13 Sep 2023 (Belgian Official Journal 2 Oct 2023) [2] |
| Death acts (incl. indexes) | **75 years** | same law – older pages that still say "50 years" are outdated [2][3] |
| Population registers | **120 years** | after the register was closed [3][10][11] |
| Archives in general | usually 100 years for records with personal data | check the inventory |

What is online: civil registers **up to 1920** for most municipalities [2]. Marriage and death acts between
c. 1920 and 75 years ago are public but, as of March 2026, **can only be consulted at the municipality**; the
transfer of the 1919–1969 registers to the State Archives has started but will take years, and digitisation with
FamilySearch has not yet begun [2]. Living people and recent records: follow `reference/privacy.md`.
For newer records a direct descendant can request an extract or copy from the municipality (fee possible) [10].

### Regions and borders
- **Flanders** (Antwerp, Limburg, East and West Flanders, Flemish Brabant): Dutch/Flemish; very strong volunteer
  indexing (Familiekunde Vlaanderen) [5].
- **Wallonia** (Hainaut, Namur, Liège, Luxembourg, Walloon Brabant): French; Liège was a prince-bishopric, not
  part of the Austrian Netherlands – old-regime institutions differ.
- **Brussels**: bilingual; 19 municipalities, each with its own registers; the City of Brussels has its own
  archives [12].
- **East Cantons** (Eupen, Malmedy, St. Vith; Prussian 1815–1920): German; civil registers 1800–1920 at the
  **State Archives in Eupen**; FamilySearch has some of them (e.g. Kelmis/Neutral Moresnet 1802–1920) [15].
- **Province of Luxembourg** (State Archives in Arlon): close ties to the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, whose
  parish registers are on **Matricula** [16]. Matricula has **no Belgian dioceses** [16].
- **Border with the Netherlands**: Zeelandic Flanders families crossed the border constantly; use the
  **Zeeuws Archief** ("Zeeuwen Gezocht", over 7 million names) and Open Archives [17].
- **Border with France**: Hainaut and West Flanders families often appear in French departmental archives
  (Nord, Pas-de-Calais) – see a France guide if available.

## 2. Portals and how to use them

### AGATHA – State Archives in Belgium (agatha.arch.be) – the main portal
The old site **search.arch.be has been retired** (it now redirects to an end-of-life notice); AGATHA replaces it
and old accounts still work [1][6][7]. Old non-permanent links could not be carried over [6].

| Search engine | What it is | Login | Download |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Genealogical sources** (*Genealogische bronnen / Sources généalogiques*) | browse digitised **parish registers (before 1796)** and **civil registers (from 1796)** by municipality – images, not names | **free account required** to open the viewer [2][6] | save page → **max. 100 pages per day** [2][6] |
| **Analyses of records** (*Analyses van akten*, formerly "Zoeken naar personen / Rechercher des personnes") | volunteer **name index**: 42 million names, 13 million records 1580–1920 – parish and civil records, notarial deeds, militia registers, foreigners' files, property records… [4] | **no login** [4] | results page as **CSV**; analysis as PDF/print; each analysis has a permanent ID and URL [4] |
| **Archive inventories** | ~20,000 inventories (notarial, court, aldermen, fiscal…) [1] | account for digitised items [6] | documents downloadable; **maps, plans and photographs cannot be downloaded** [6] |

**Genealogical sources – step by step** (user guide 03/2026 [2]):
1. Choose the tab **Parish registers (before 1796)** or **Civil status registers (as from 1796)** – you cannot
   search both at once.
2. Pick the **municipality** (pre-1977 name; "List of municipalities" and "Search by parish" help).
3. Select the parish(es) if a town had several.
4. Tick the record types (Births/Baptisms, Marriages, Deaths/Funerals) and whether you want **records,
   indexes or both**. Tick indexes first.
5. Set the period ("In", "From/until", "Before", "After") and **Search**. Results: register lines with
   **View**. "Not available" = missing register (hover **Info** for why). "Mixed certificates" = births,
   marriages and deaths in one book.
6. **View** opens the viewer (only when logged in): table of contents left, thumbnails at the bottom, page number
   field, zoom, brightness/contrast, rotate. A page can be saved (downloaded) and printed – up to 100 a day.
7. The filter **Date of update** shows digitisation progress.

**Analyses of records – tips** [4]: search the surname alone first (more criteria = fewer hits); wildcards `*`
and `?` (`De ?oninck` → De Coninck / De Koninck); first names are **not translated** – search Antoine, Antoon and
Antonius separately; one role and one record type per search; you can add a second person (e.g. both spouses).
Volunteers make mistakes – **always open the original register** before you record the fact.

### FamilySearch (free account)
- Per-province collections, e.g. "Belgium, East Flanders, Civil Registration, 1541-1950" (≈ 2.9 million images,
  partly indexed, including marriage supplements and original indexes), Antwerp 1588-1953, Brabant 1704-1916,
  Hainaut 1600-1913, Liège 1621-1914, Limburg 1798-1906, Luxembourg 1580-1920, Namur 1800-1912, West Flanders
  1582-1950 [8].
- Nationwide indexes: Belgium Births and Baptisms 1560-1890, Marriages 1563-1890, Deaths and Burials 1564-1900
  (index only) [9][18]; "Belgium, Police Immigration, 1840-1930" (images) [18].
- **Catalog** (familysearch.org/search/catalog): search by place "Belgium, <province>, <municipality>" for
  population registers, notarial records and other films [10]. Some films are restricted to FamilySearch
  centres or affiliate libraries because of contracts [9][15].
- The user must log in personally. Images can be downloaded with the viewer's download button when the film is
  not restricted.

## 3. Languages, calendar and names

Records are in **French** (all of Belgium 1796–c.1815/1823, Wallonia and Brussels always, many Flemish towns
until late 19th c.), **Dutch** (Flanders), **Latin** (parish registers) and **German** (East Cantons). The
genealogical meaning is the same – learn the key words.

### The French Republican calendar (1796 – 31 Dec 1805)
A year (*an*) starts on 1 Vendémiaire; 12 months of 30 days, then 5 (in leap years 6) *jours complémentaires*.
Month order: Vendémiaire, Brumaire, Frimaire, Nivôse, Pluviôse, Ventôse, Germinal, Floréal, Prairial, Messidor,
Thermidor, Fructidor. The Gregorian calendar returned on 1 January 1806 (= 11 Nivôse XIV).

| Year | 1 Vendémiaire = | Year | 1 Vendémiaire = |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV | 23 Sep 1795 | X | 23 Sep 1801 |
| V | 22 Sep 1796 | XI | 23 Sep 1802 |
| VI | 22 Sep 1797 | XII | 24 Sep 1803 |
| VII | 22 Sep 1798 | XIII | 23 Sep 1804 |
| VIII | 23 Sep 1799 | XIV | 23 Sep 1805 |
| IX | 23 Sep 1800 | | |

Conversion: Gregorian date = (1 Vendémiaire of that year) + (month − 1) × 30 + (day − 1) days; complementary days
count as month 13. Claude should compute this in Python, not by hand, and record both forms, e.g.
`* 15 Oct 1797 (24 Vendémiaire VI)`. Online check: Fourmilab calendar converter [27].

### Names
- **Women keep their maiden name** in all records – search the wife under her own surname.
- **Flemish surnames** often come from patronymics in the genitive (*Janssens* = Jan's son, *Peeters, Claes,
  Hendrickx*), places (*Van Damme, Van den Bossche*) and occupations (*De Smet, De Backer, Vermeulen*). Spelling is
  fluid: *De Smet / De Smedt / Desmet*, *Verhaeghe / Verhaege*, *Van de Velde / Vandevelde* – search with
  wildcards and read, do not trust spelling.
- **Walloon names**: *dit* names (aliases) occur; spelling varies (*Dupont / Dupond*).
- **First names change language** with the clerk: Latin in baptisms, French or Dutch in civil acts.
  Joannes = Jan / Jean / Johann; Petrus = Pieter / Pierre; Jacobus = Jacob, Jaak / Jacques; Judocus = Joos /
  Josse; Aegidius = Gillis / Gilles; Livinus = Lieven; Franciscus = Frans / François; Carolus = Karel / Charles;
  Ludovicus = Lodewijk / Louis; Guilielmus = Willem / Guillaume; Joanna = Johanna / Jeanne; Livina = Lieve;
  Coleta = Colette. Several children may get the same name after a sibling died.

## 4. Research workflow for Belgium

Example family (fictional): Pierre Dubois from Ghent, born about 1880.

1. **Family first** (Phase 1 of SKILL.md): names, dates, places, *bidprentjes* (memorial cards), death notices,
   marriage booklets (*trouwboekje / livret de mariage*), military papers. The memorial card of a grandparent
   often gives exact birth and death dates and the spouse.
2. **Start from the youngest event older than the closing periods** (birth > 100 years, marriage/death > 75):
   newer acts need the municipality; online material mostly ends in **1920** [2].
3. **10-year index → act.** In AGATHA open the civil register index for the municipality and decade, find the
   name and year/act number, then open that year's register and read the act. Save the page (count toward the
   100/day limit), crop the entry, record it (`tools/write.py`, kind `B`/`M`/`D`).
4. **Marriage act first.** It names both sets of parents, ages and birthplaces. If a parent is "deceased", the
   **marriage annexes** (FamilySearch for some provinces; otherwise State Archives reading room) contain the
   death act; the birth extracts give the birthplace when it was elsewhere.
5. **Population registers** (from 1846): put the household together, find siblings, see where the family came
   from and went – then search those municipalities' indexes.
6. **Climb through births and marriages** to 1796. Check the Republican-calendar dates.
7. **Parish registers before 1796.** Use the AGATHA indexes (many registers have 19th-century name indexes)
   and the **Analyses of records** name search; then read baptisms (godparents are often grandparents, uncles,
   aunts – record them, `tools/godparents.py`), marriages (often in the bride's parish!) and burials.
8. **When the trail breaks:** check neighbouring parishes (marriage in the bride's parish), chapels of ease,
   the town's other parishes, then section 6 below: *staten van goed*, aldermen's registers, notarial deeds,
   militia, fiscal and land records.
9. **Record everything with sources** (section 5) and write down negative results only with evidence (which
   register, which years, which pages were read).

## 5. Practical notes for automation (Claude)

- **Network brake:** every scripted request goes through `tools/polite.py` (see SKILL.md, "Being gentle with
  archives"). There are **no Belgium-specific download tools** in the skill yet – AGATHA and FamilySearch are
  browser work.
- **AGATHA Genealogical sources:** needs the user's own free account. **Never ask for or type the password** –
  the user logs in personally in the Playwright browser window, then Claude navigates. Save pages through the
  viewer's download button (`download.saveAs` in Playwright); respect the **100 downloads per day** limit [2][6]
  – plan which pages you need, read the index first, and screenshot the zoomed entry instead of downloading when
  you only need to read. Pace: a page every few seconds, no parallel helpers on the same site.
- **AGATHA Analyses of records:** works without login [4]; result pages can be exported as CSV one page at a
  time; you cannot jump more than 4 result pages – narrow the search with filters instead [4].
- **FamilySearch:** the user logs in personally; images through the browser (Playwright `download.saveAs`).
  Do not scrape search results in bulk.
- **Open Archives, Geneanet forum:** returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching in our tests (Nov 2026) – use the
  browser and treat them as manual sources.
- **BelgicaPress / hetarchief.be:** free browsing for pre-1918 issues [21][22]; no login needed for those.
- **Citing an image** – write it so anyone can find the page again:
  `State Archives in Belgium, Ghent repository · civil registration Ghent · births 1880 · act no. 1234 ·
  image 312 of 980 · <AGATHA viewer or permalink URL> · accessed 18 Nov 2026`.
  For FamilySearch: collection name, film/DGS number, image number, ark: permalink. For an index (Analyses of
  records) cite its record identifier (e.g. `GRIMPR_GRIMBEG_00026058`) [4] – and mark it as an index, then find the
  original. In `volumes.json` store archive = repository, title = municipality + record type + years, url;
  in `records.json` store `image` and `link` (see `reference/data.md`). Check that the link opens without your
  session; if not, also store the register title and image number.

## 6. Beyond civil and parish registers – sources that often break dead ends

Belgian equivalents of the "other sources" that help most when the registers stop. Online status was checked
where possible; much of this material is **only in reading rooms** or as finding aids (AGATHA "Archive
inventories"). General approach for Claude: (1) find the right institution and series in the AGATHA archive
inventories by municipality; (2) search the **Analyses of records** – volunteers have indexed many notarial,
property, militia and foreigners' files [4]; (3) check Familiekunde Vlaanderen indexes [5] and the local
heritage society; (4) only then plan a reading-room visit or a request.

### Land and property
| Source | What it tells you | Period | Online? | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aldermen's registers** – *schepenregisters, schepenbrieven, wettelijke passeringen* (Flanders), *gichten* (Limburg/Brabant area), *œuvres de loi* (Wallonia; unverified term use) | sales, mortgages, divisions of inheritance before the local aldermen's bench (voluntary jurisdiction) – heirs are named with their relationships | 15th c. – 1795 | finding aids on AGATHA ("Wettelijke passeringen" listed per *schepenbank*) [28]; some municipal indexes (e.g. Roeselare city archive search) [28]; images rarely online | an inheritance division lists all children and their spouses – the Belgian counterpart of a Czech land book |
| *cijnsboeken, landboeken, terriers / rentiers* (rent and land rolls) | who held which plot and paid rent to a lord or church | 14th – 18th c. | mostly reading room (unverified per collection) | follow one holding across generations |
| **Feudal registers** – *leenboeken / registres aux reliefs* | succession to fiefs: on a death the heir "relieved" the fief – gives death and heir | medieval – 1795 | reading room (unverified) | useful for richer farmers and lesser nobility |
| **Ferraris map** (*Kabinetskaart der Oostenrijkse Nederlanden*) | detailed map of farms, mills, roads | 1771–1778 | Geopunt (Flanders) historical map layers (unverified exact layer) | locate the farm named in an 18th-century act |
| **Primitive cadastre** (*primitief kadaster*, legger / matrice) | owners and parcels | c. 1808–1834 (unverified dates) | State Archives cadastre fonds; reading room | link a family to a parcel number |
| **Popp maps** (*Atlas cadastral parcellaire de la Belgique*) | parcel map + register of owners per municipality | 1842–1879 | Flanders: scanned and georeferenced, viewable in Geopunt, WMTS `https://geo.api.vlaanderen.be/HISTCART/wmts` layer `popp` [29]; State Archives images linked to descriptions [30]; **never made for Limburg, Luxembourg and Namur** [30] | owner names on the map → house of the population register |

### Tax and census lists (counterparts of the Czech *berní rula*)

### Orphans and guardianship (counterpart of Czech *sirotčí registra*)
- **Staten van goed** (estate inventories) were drawn up when a parent died leaving minor children; they list
  the surviving spouse, the children (often with ages), guardians from the father's and mother's side, debts and
  property – and sometimes the grandparents [32]. Mostly Flanders, 16th–18th c.; kept in aldermen's archives
  (*schepenbanken*, e.g. Kasselrij Oudenaarde). Many are digitised in the State Archives inventories and indexed
  by regions (e.g. Meetjesland, Land van Nevele, *Index Staten van Goed Brugse Vrije*) [5][32].
- **Orphan books / orphan masters** (*wezenboeken, weesmeesters*), guardianship accounts (*voogdijrekeningen,
  wezenrekeningen*) – same archives [32]. After 1796 guardianship goes to the justice of the peace
  (*vredegerecht / justice de paix*) – reading room (unverified per court).
- *Oppergriffie* – the meaning of this term could **not be verified**; ask the State Archives before relying on it.
- **Tip:** a *staat van goed* is the best way past a burial without age or a parish register gap; search the
  Analyses of records for the surname with record type "inventory of property".

### Disease, poverty and death causes
- Civil death acts usually give **no cause of death**. Clusters of deaths in one family within weeks point to
  epidemics: cholera hit Belgium in 1832–33, 1848–49, 1854, 1859 and worst in 1866; the Spanish flu in 1918
  (years from general history – unverified per locality). Read the whole month in the register: many deaths of the
  same age group confirm an epidemic – a good story for the website (`reference/stories.md`).
- **Poor relief**: before 1796 the parish *armentafel* / *Heilige-Geesttafel* (Holy Spirit table); from 1796 the
  *Bureau de bienfaisance / Bureel van Weldadigheid* and hospice commissions; merged in 1925 into the commissions
  that became today's **OCMW / CPAS** [35]. Their archives (lists of the poor, accounts, sometimes foster care) are
  in the State Archives or the OCMW/CPAS archive; gaps are common [35].
- **Foundlings**: e.g. the OCMW Brussels foundling archive [35]. Hospital and *godshuis* (almshouse) records sit
  in the same archives.

### Local history and chronicles (counterpart of Czech village chronicles)
- **Heritage societies** (*heemkundige kringen / cercles d'histoire locale*) publish journals with transcribed
  sources, house histories and lists; many journals are deposited at the Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience
  (Antwerp) via Heemkunde Vlaanderen [36]. Search "heemkundige kring <municipality>".
- **Parish chronicles** (*liber memorialis*), school and convent chronicles – parish or diocesan archives
  (unverified online availability).
- **Regional heritage banks** (*erfgoedbanken, beeldbanken*) with photos, memorial cards and local documents
  exist for many regions [36] – no central list was verified.
- **Newspapers**: BelgicaPress [21], hetarchief.be [22], Delpher (Dutch newspapers and books, incl. many
  digitised Ghent university books) [37].

### Other sources
- **Notarial deeds** (marriage contracts, wills, sales, divisions): from the 16th century; older notarial
  archives at the State Archives; many indexed in the Analyses of records ("notarial deeds") [4]; inventories on
  AGATHA. Marriage contracts name parents and property; wills name heirs.
- **Court records**: aldermen's benches (civil and criminal), the **Council of Flanders** (*Raad van Vlaanderen*,
  State Archives Ghent), Council of Brabant, Great Council of Mechelen – reading room, inventories online.
- **Guild records** (*ambachten / métiers*): masters and apprentices in towns – city archives.
- **Emigration**: the Red Star Line (Antwerp → USA/Canada, 1873–1934) carried about 2 million passengers, but its
  departure lists are reported to be **largely lost** (forum report – unverified) [38]; use US/Canadian arrival
  lists and the **population register** departure notes instead. Foreigners *in* Belgium: Antwerp foreigners'
  files 1840–1930 (index on Open Archives, images on FamilySearch) [18][19], Foreigners' Police files 1835–1912
  (State Archives) [39]. Emigration to Argentina, Brazil, Guatemala colony etc. – unverified, search population
  registers and newspapers.
- **Seasonal workers** (Flemish *Fransmans* to northern France, Walloon workers to French farms and mines):
  absences show in population registers; deaths abroad were often transcribed into the home municipality's
  register (unverified as a rule).
- **Memorial cards** (*bidprentjes / souvenirs mortuaires*) and **death notices** (*rouwbrieven / lettres de
  faire-part*): huge collections at Familiekunde Vlaanderen centres and branches (Westkust, Tielt, Bruges,
  Dilbeek, Leuven, Ostend…) [5], some on Open Archives and Geneanet [40]; ask the family first – almost every
  Flemish family has a box of *bidprentjes*.
- **Photos and gravestones**: local heritage banks; gravestone projects (unverified for Belgium).
